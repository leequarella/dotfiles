# Lattice's guidance on effective peer feedback

Distilled from Lattice's own published guidance (lattice.com/articles and lattice.com/library) on what makes feedback useful versus what makes it forgettable. Read this before drafting either a request or a response — the same principles apply to both, since a well-written question and a well-written answer are two halves of the same specific, evidence-based exchange.

## The core principles

**Be specific and evidence-based.** Feedback is inherently subjective, so the fix is to tie it to something that actually happened. A question or answer that references a real project, thread, or moment is inherently more useful than one that could apply to anyone.

**Make it actionable.** Good feedback answers "what exactly should I do differently (or keep doing)?" Feedback that only says someone should "step it up" gives them nothing to act on — it's the equivalent of a New Year's resolution to "lose weight" instead of "walk 20 minutes at lunch." Note also that this failure mode disproportionately lands on people from underrepresented groups, who are statistically more likely to receive feedback that's vague and non-actionable — so specificity isn't just a nice-to-have, it's a fairness issue.

**Focus on behaviors, not just results.** A result ("we missed the deadline") is downstream of specific, controllable actions. Naming the behavior is what makes the feedback useful: not "you didn't hit the target" but "I noticed the follow-up emails to the three key clients went out a week later than planned."

**Be concrete and quantifiable where you can.** "Good feedback is specific and quantifiable." Vague feedback can't be acted on because there's nothing to put an action against.

**Structure matters, for praise as much as for critique.** A loose, rambling answer is harder to act on than one with a clear shape: what happened, what it looked like, what the effect was.

Source: [How to Write a Peer Review: Giving Constructive Feedback](https://lattice.com/articles/how-to-write-a-peer-review), [How to Give Meaningful Feedback (With Examples)](https://lattice.com/library/how-to-give-meaningful-feedback-with-examples), [Tips for Making Feedback More Equitable](https://lattice.com/articles/3-tips-for-making-feedback-more-equitable)

## Turning a vague question into a specific one

| Vague (avoid) | Specific (aim for) |
|---|---|
| How am I doing on communication? | When we were coordinating the [project] launch in [month], did the updates I posted in [channel/doc] give you what you needed, or was something missing? |
| Any feedback on my collaboration? | During the [project] crunch, I leaned on you to cover [specific task]. Did that feel like a fair split, or did it create friction? |
| How's my leadership? | When [specific decision] came up on [project], how did I handle bringing the team along on it — too fast, too slow, about right? |

## Question bank, by theme

Use these as scaffolding, not scripts — always swap in the real project/thread/moment found in the evidence-gathering step. A question with no specific noun in it hasn't been adapted yet.

**Collaboration / working together**
- When we worked on [project] together, was there a moment where my approach made your job easier, or harder?
- Did I pull my weight on [specific shared task], or did you end up covering more than you should have?

**Communication**
- Looking back at [thread/doc/meeting], was the way I communicated [decision/update] clear enough, or did it need more context than I gave?
- Is there a moment where you wished I'd looped you in earlier or later than I did?

**Growth / skill development**
- On [project], where do you think I'm closest to leveling up, and what's still holding me back?
- What's one thing you saw me do this cycle that you'd want to see more of?

**Technical / domain skill**
- On [specific deliverable], how did the quality of my work land compared to what the situation needed?
- Was there a technical or domain call I made on [project] that you'd have made differently?

**Leadership / influence**
- When [specific decision or conflict] came up, how did I handle bringing others along?
- Did I create space for your input on [project], or did it feel more one-directional?

## Applying this to a response, not just a request

The same table works in reverse. If someone asks "how did I do on communication this cycle," the weak answer is "great, very clear." The strong answer names the actual thread: "the update you posted in [doc/channel] before the [date] deadline was exactly the kind of heads-up that let me replan my week — more of that, less of the quieter stretches where I had to ask what was going on."
